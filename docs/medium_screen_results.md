## Interpretation

All eight planned runs completed without a timeout or execution failure, using
2.80 minutes of summed solver time (loading and initial CPU audits are additional).

* On final-3068, guarded multi-shift reached 5.60% lower cost than guarded single
  shift, but took 18.87 versus 7.84 seconds. Rebuild retries fell from 34 to zero;
  multi-shift still needed 28 backtracking rescues. Zero rebuild retries does
  not mean every unscaled menu succeeded.
* On Venice-1672, guarded multi-shift reached 1.90% lower cost in 42.27 versus
  39.51 seconds; neither configuration rebuilt a rejected attempt.
* Caspar FP32 completed in 0.82 and 5.16 seconds, stopping before its 200-iteration
  cap. Its checked endpoint costs were substantially higher. Compare cost-matched
  crossings above, not just the unequal-budget runtimes.
* Rearming fired **zero times** on both scenes. The multi/rearm differences do
  not demonstrate a rearming benefit; their policies did not diverge through
  that mechanism in this short window. One-repeat floating-point trajectories
  and timings differ even without activating the experimental policy.

This screen supports a useful early quality/runtime tradeoff for multi-shift.
It does not establish full-convergence performance or validate the late-retry
fix on medium inputs. No extra long runs were launched; the broad queue remains
paused. Frozen binary hashes were verified unchanged, all PRISM traces were
monotone, and their initial fp64 objectives matched the independent scorer.
